About & Purpose

Our Mission

Why this project exists, who it serves, and what we aim to do.

Everyone deserves to feel safe and respected in their community. AAPI SafeSpace exists to raise awareness, connect people with support, and build more informed and inclusive communities — because education and solidarity are the foundation of lasting change.

— Kenneth Kong, AAPI SafeSpace Project

The Issue

Anti-Asian hate and discrimination have risen sharply in recent years, including verbal harassment, stereotyping, and physical violence. Despite increased awareness, many incidents go unreported due to fear, language barriers, and distrust in reporting systems. The COVID-19 pandemic intensified harmful stereotypes and rhetoric that put AAPI communities at greater risk.

Who We Serve

This project focuses on the AAPI community — including students, immigrants, and individuals who may face cultural or language barriers when seeking help. Hate incidents affect mental health, sense of safety, and willingness to participate in public life. Many individuals feel isolated or unsupported after experiencing discrimination.

Why It Matters

Technology can play a powerful role in reducing barriers by centralizing information, connecting people to resources, and educating communities. But it can also spread harm through misinformation and hate speech. This website uses technology intentionally — to uplift voices, spread awareness, and make support easier to find.

What This Website Does

AAPI SafeSpace is an advocacy website that brings together research, resources, and education in one accessible place. It is designed to raise awareness about anti-Asian discrimination, connect individuals to real support services, and empower bystanders to take informed action — all built around the needs of the AAPI community and its allies.

Research

What the Studies Show

Three key sources grounding this project in evidence-based understanding of anti-AAPI hate.

01

Stop AAPI Hate National Report (Through December 31, 2021)

Jeung, R., Yellow Horse, A., Popovic, T., & Lim, R. — 2022 · Stop AAPI Hate

This foundational report documents over 10,000 hate incidents submitted to the Stop AAPI Hate reporting system — incidents that, without digital tools, would likely never have been recorded. The report highlights how technology lowers barriers to reporting by giving individuals a safer and more convenient way to share their experiences. The centralized data reveals patterns in discrimination and has been used to advocate for meaningful policy changes at the local, state, and national level. Digital reporting tools are especially important for those without access to in-person support services.

02

#StopAsianHate on TikTok: A Content Analysis of Social Media Activism

Jacques, E. T. — 2022 · Journal of Medical Internet Research

This peer-reviewed study analyzed 100 TikTok videos using the #StopAsianHate hashtag and found that more than half focused on raising awareness and educating viewers about discrimination. Social media platforms allow marginalized voices to reach large audiences, helping people feel less alone and encouraging more community members to speak out. The research also found that videos specifically focused on awareness and incidents of abuse were more likely to be shared, demonstrating how social media activism can amplify advocacy at scale. This study directly informed the website's design approach: education and storytelling are essential tools for change.

View Source ↗
03

Anti-Asian Hate Crime During COVID-19: Exploring the Role of Online Rhetoric

Han, S. — 2022 · Journal of Interpersonal Violence

This study examines how online rhetoric during the COVID-19 pandemic contributed to a measurable rise in anti-Asian hate crimes. Misinformation and harmful language spread rapidly through digital platforms, reinforcing stereotypes and normalizing discrimination. Because content can be shared instantly, harmful narratives reached large audiences quickly — and research by He et al. (2020) additionally shows that exposure to racist content online increases the likelihood of adopting similar attitudes. This dual role of technology — as both a tool for harm and a tool for healing — is central to why AAPI SafeSpace is designed to prioritize education, transparency, and safety in equal measure.

📌 Websites & Organizations
Reporting

Stop AAPI Hate

The nation's largest reporting center tracking hate and discrimination against Asian Americans and Pacific Islanders. Offers multilingual resources, policy advocacy, and community toolkits for individuals, allies, and organizations.

stopaapihate.org ↗
Legal Aid

Asian Americans Advancing Justice (AAJC)

One of the nation's largest civil rights organizations serving AAPI communities. Provides free or low-cost legal advice, litigation support, immigration resources, and bystander intervention training in partnership with Right To Be (formerly Hollaback!).

advancingjustice-aajc.org ↗
Mental Health

Asian Mental Health Collective (AMHC)

Focused on de-stigmatizing mental health within the global Asian community. Provides the APISAA Therapist Directory (therapists in most U.S. states specializing in Asian American communities), a community blog, and a directory of mental health organizations.

asianmhc.org ↗
Mental Health

NAAPIMHA

The National Asian American Pacific Islander Mental Health Association promotes mental health and wellbeing for AAPI communities. Offers a resource list of state-level programs and mental health services available in all 50 states, along with general wellness resources.

naapimha.org ↗
Bystander Training

Right To Be (formerly Hollaback!)

A global movement to end harassment. Offers free bystander intervention training co-sponsored with Advancing Justice AAJC, teaching individuals how to safely intervene when they witness anti-Asian harassment — online and in person.

righttobe.org ↗
